About EcoDairy & Methodology

Understanding carbon footprint analysis for dairy farms

What is a Carbon Footprint?

A carbon footprint is the total amount of greenhouse gases (GHGs) produced directly and indirectly by an activity, organisation, event, or product. It is measured in units of carbon dioxide equivalent (CO₂e), which allows different greenhouse gases to be compared on a common scale based on their global warming potential.

For a dairy farm, the carbon footprint includes emissions from the cattle themselves (enteric fermentation and manure), energy use (electricity and diesel), and the production and transport of purchased feed.

What is CO₂ Equivalent (CO₂e)?

CO₂ equivalent is a metric used to express the impact of different greenhouse gases in terms of the amount of CO₂ that would create the same amount of warming. This uses Global Warming Potential (GWP) values:

  • Carbon dioxide (CO₂): GWP = 1 (reference gas)
  • Methane (CH₄): GWP ≈ 28 over 100 years — methane is about 28 times more potent than CO₂
  • Nitrous oxide (N₂O): GWP ≈ 265 over 100 years

When we say "1,500 kg CO₂e per cow," this means the methane (and other gases) from one cow have the same warming effect as 1,500 kg of CO₂.

Why Do Dairy Farms Produce GHG Emissions?

Dairy farms are significant sources of greenhouse gases due to several interconnected biological and operational processes:

  • Enteric Fermentation: Cows are ruminant animals with a specialized digestive system. Microorganisms in their rumen break down cellulose-rich plant material, producing methane as a by-product. This is the single largest source of emissions on most dairy farms.
  • Manure Management: When manure decomposes, especially under anaerobic (oxygen-free) conditions in storage lagoons or heaps, it produces methane and nitrous oxide.
  • Energy Use: Farms consume electricity for milking machines, refrigeration, lighting, and water pumps, and diesel for tractors, feed transport, and field operations.
  • Feed Production: Growing, processing, and transporting cattle feed involves energy use, fertiliser application, and land-use change, all of which generate emissions.

Emission Sources Explained

1. Enteric Methane

Enteric methane refers to the CH₄ produced during the normal digestive process of ruminant animals. A single dairy cow can produce 70–120 kg of methane per year, which translates to roughly 1,960–3,360 kg CO₂e using a GWP of 28. In this model, we use a default of 1,500 kg CO₂e per cow per year as an illustrative value.

2. Manure Management

Manure-management emissions arise when livestock waste decomposes. The emissions depend on how manure is stored and handled — liquid systems (lagoons) produce more methane than solid systems or pasture-deposited manure. Our default is 200 kg CO₂e per cow per year.

3. Electricity

Electricity emissions depend on both the amount consumed and the emission factor of the electricity grid. Regions with more coal-fired power have higher emission factors (0.8–1.0 kg CO₂e/kWh), while regions with more renewables have lower factors (0.1–0.4 kg CO₂e/kWh). Our default is 0.70 kg CO₂e/kWh.

4. Diesel

Diesel combustion releases CO₂ directly. The standard emission factor is approximately 2.68 kg CO₂e per litre, accounting for both direct combustion emissions and a small upstream component.

5. Feed-Related Emissions

These cover the emissions embedded in purchased feed — from fertiliser production, field operations, processing, and transport. The emission factor varies widely by feed type and origin. Our default of 0.50 kg CO₂e/kg is a simplified average for illustrative purposes.

Calculation Methodology

This tool uses a transparent, activity-based methodology. Each emission source is calculated separately using the formula:

Enteric Emissions = Number of Cows × Methane per Cow (kg CO₂e)

Electricity Emissions = Electricity Consumed (kWh) × Electricity EF (kg CO₂e/kWh)

Diesel Emissions = Diesel Consumed (L) × Diesel EF (kg CO₂e/L)

Feed Emissions = Feed Purchased (kg) × Feed EF (kg CO₂e/kg)

Manure Emissions = Number of Cows × Manure per Cow (kg CO₂e)

Total Emissions = Enteric + Electricity + Diesel + Feed + Manure

Total (tonnes) = Total (kg) ÷ 1,000

Emissions Saved = Baseline Emissions − Scenario Emissions

% Reduction = (Emissions Saved ÷ Baseline Emissions) × 100

Projection Method

Projected Activity (Year n) = Current Activity × (1 + Annual Growth Rate)ⁿ

Projected Emissions = Σ (Projected Activity × Emission Factor) for each source

Assumptions & Limitations

  • All emission factors are illustrative educational assumptions, not official or region-specific values.
  • Excluded sources: Carbon sequestration by pasture or trees, construction/building emissions, embodied emissions in purchased equipment, transport of products to market, and animal feed grown on-farm.
  • The model uses a static emission factor approach — real-world emission factors may change over time.
  • No financial cost or return-on-investment analysis is included.
  • Projections use simple compound-growth assumptions and do not account for market changes, policy, or climate variability.
  • Feed improvement impacts on methane are highly variable; the 0–20% range is a simplified educational estimate.
  • This tool is designed for educational demonstration and should not be used for regulatory reporting or commercial decision-making.

Sustainable Livestock Management

Sustainable livestock management aims to balance productivity with environmental responsibility. Key strategies include:

  • Improved feed efficiency: Better nutrition can reduce methane per unit of milk produced.
  • Manure management: Anaerobic digesters can capture methane for energy while reducing emissions.
  • Renewable energy: Solar panels and wind turbines can power farm operations with lower emissions.
  • Operational efficiency: Regular machinery maintenance, route optimization, and energy-efficient equipment reduce energy waste.
  • Herd management: Genetic selection, animal health programs, and optimised herd size can improve emissions intensity per litre of milk.

Every farm is unique — sustainable improvements should be evaluated with qualified agricultural and environmental advisors who understand local conditions.

Project Information

This project — "EcoDairy: Smart Carbon Footprint Analyzer for Dairy Farms" — is a microproject for the Environmental Studies (EVS) course for Information Science & Engineering students.

It demonstrates how ISE concepts — data processing, mathematical modeling, visualization, interactive software development, and decision-support systems — can be applied to a real environmental problem.

Case Study: Lakshmi owns a dairy farm with 10 cows. She wants to understand the farm's annual carbon footprint and discover ways to reduce its environmental impact without compromising milk production or operations.

Technology Stack: HTML5, CSS3, JavaScript, Chart.js, Lucide Icons, LocalStorage.
